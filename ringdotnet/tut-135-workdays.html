Appendix

Quick introduction to Ring

Working days

Business programs count days that are not weekends and not holidays, group dates into weeks and quarters, and compute ages. This lesson adds working days to a date, counts them between two dates, finds ISO week numbers and the end of a month, and computes an age.

What you will learn#

  • DayOfWeek and a weekend that is kept in one place,
  • adding working days: a loop over AddDays(1) that skips weekends and holidays,
  • counting working days between two dates,
  • ISO week numbers and why week 1 can start in December,
  • quarters and DaysInMonth (with leap years),
  • an age.

The tutorial Dates and times and the tutorial Calendars and dates showed the date types and the calendar of a month. This lesson is about the rules that business programs add.

The program#

samples/UsingDotNet/tutorials/ch03_workdays.ring
# working days - adding business days, skipping weekends and holidays, week numbers, quarters, month ends and an age
load "dotnet.ring"

using {
	"System"
	"System.Globalization"
}

# the holidays of this example (fixed dates written as year-month-day)
aHolidays = [ "2030-01-01", "2030-12-25", "2030-12-26" ]

func main

	# 1. The day of the week. DayOfWeek is a text: "Monday" ... "Sunday". Many countries have another weekend (Friday and Saturday), so the
	#    weekend is a list in one place.
	oDate = makeDate(2030,3,9)
	? "1. " + oDate.Format("yyyy-MM-dd") + " is a " + oDate.DayOfWeek + ", weekend: " + yesNo(isWeekend(oDate))
	? "   the days of the week of 11-17 March 2030:"
	cLine = ""
	for i = 11 to 17
		cLine += makeDate(2030,3,i).Format("ddd d") + "  "
	next
	? "   " + cLine

	# 2. Adding days is simple (AddDays), and adding WORKING days is a loop that skips the weekend and the holidays
	oStart = makeDate(2030,3,7)                         # a Thursday
	? nl + "2. from Thursday " + oStart.Format("yyyy-MM-dd") + ":"
	? "   + 1 calendar day:  " + oStart.AddDays(1).Format("ddd yyyy-MM-dd")
	for nDays in [ 1, 2, 5, 10 ]
		? "   + " + padRight("" + nDays,2) + " working days: " + addWorkdays(oStart,nDays).Format("ddd yyyy-MM-dd")
	next

	# 3. Holidays move the result. 2030-12-24 is a Tuesday; the 25th and 26th are holidays, and the 28th and 29th the weekend.
	oEve = makeDate(2030,12,24)
	? nl + "3. holidays: 3 working days after " + oEve.Format("yyyy-MM-dd") + " is " + addWorkdays(oEve,3).Format("ddd yyyy-MM-dd") + " (the 25th, 26th, 28th and 29th do not count)"

	# 4. Counting the working days between two dates (the first day is included, the last is not)
	? nl + "4. working days from 2030-03-01 to 2030-04-01: " + countWorkdays(makeDate(2030,3,1),makeDate(2030,4,1))
	? "   working days in the year 2030: " + countWorkdays(makeDate(2030,1,1),makeDate(2031,1,1))

	# 5. Week numbers. The ISO week starts on Monday, and week 1 is the week with the first Thursday of the year: so the first days of
	#    January can belong to the last week of the year before, and the last days of December to week 1 of the next year.
	? nl + "5. ISO weeks:"
	for aItem in [ [2030,1,1], [2030,3,9], [2030,12,30], [2027,1,1] ]
		oD = makeDate(aItem[1],aItem[2],aItem[3])
		? "   " + oD.Format("yyyy-MM-dd ddd") + "  week " + isoWeek(oD) + " of " + isoYear(oD)
	next

	# 6. Quarters and the end of a month. DaysInMonth knows the leap years.
	? nl + "6. quarters and month ends:"
	for nMonth = 1 to 12 step 4
		? "   month " + padRight("" + nMonth,3) + "quarter " + (floor((nMonth - 1) / 3) + 1) + ", the month has " + daysInMonth(2030,nMonth) + " days"
	next
	? "   February has " + daysInMonth(2030,2) + " days in 2030 and " + daysInMonth(2032,2) + " in 2032"
	? "   the last day of February 2032 is " + makeDate(2032,2,daysInMonth(2032,2)).Format("yyyy-MM-dd ddd")

	# 7. An age: the years between a birth date and a day, one less when the birthday of this year did not come yet
	? nl + "7. ages on 2030-03-09:"
	for aItem in [ [2000,3,9], [2000,3,10], [1990,12,31], [2030,3,9] ]
		? "   born " + padRight(makeDate(aItem[1],aItem[2],aItem[3]).Format("yyyy-MM-dd"),11) + "age " + ageOn(makeDate(aItem[1],aItem[2],aItem[3]),makeDate(2030,3,9))
	next

func makeDate nYear,nMonth,nDay

	return new DateTime(nYear,nMonth,nDay)

# the weekend in one place (Saturday and Sunday here; use ["Friday","Saturday"] for the countries with that weekend)
func isWeekend oDate

	cDay = "" + oDate.DayOfWeek
	return cDay = "Saturday" or cDay = "Sunday"

func isHoliday oDate

	return find(aHolidays,oDate.Format("yyyy-MM-dd")) > 0

func isWorkday oDate

	return not isWeekend(oDate) and not isHoliday(oDate)

# n working days after a date (the date itself does not count)
func addWorkdays oDate,nDays

	oCurrent = oDate
	nLeft = nDays
	while nLeft > 0
		oCurrent = oCurrent.AddDays(1)
		if isWorkday(oCurrent)
			nLeft--
		ok
	end
	return oCurrent

func countWorkdays oFrom,oTo

	nCount = 0
	oCurrent = oFrom
	while oCurrent.CompareTo(oTo) < 0
		if isWorkday(oCurrent)
			nCount++
		ok
		oCurrent = oCurrent.AddDays(1)
	end
	return nCount

func isoWeek oDate

	return new ISOWeek().GetWeekOfYear(oDate)

func isoYear oDate

	return new ISOWeek().GetYear(oDate)

func daysInMonth nYear,nMonth

	return new DateTime(nYear,nMonth,1).DaysInMonth(nYear,nMonth)

# the years between two dates, minus one when the birthday of that year has not come yet
func ageOn oBirth,oDay

	nAge = oDay.Year - oBirth.Year
	if oDay.Month < oBirth.Month or (oDay.Month = oBirth.Month and oDay.Day < oBirth.Day)
		nAge--
	ok
	return nAge

func yesNo lValue

	if lValue
		return "yes"
	ok
	return "no"

func padRight cText,nWidth

	return cText + copy(" ",nWidth - len(cText))

How it works#

The weekend and the holidays#

DayOfWeek gives the name of the day as a text. The function isWeekend compares it with "Saturday" and "Sunday"; some countries have a weekend on Friday and Saturday, and then only this function changes. The holidays are a Ring list of dates written as year-month-day, and isHoliday looks for the date in it. Real programs keep the holidays in a file or a table for each country and year (and some holidays, like Easter, move: they come from a rule, not from a fixed date).

Adding working days#

There is no formula that handles weekends and holidays, so addWorkdays does what a person does: it steps one day at a time and counts only the days that are working days. From a Thursday, 2 working days is the next Monday. The date itself does not count. Around 25 December 2030 (a Wednesday) the result jumps over the holidays and the weekend: 3 working days after the 24th is the 31st.

Counting between two dates#

countWorkdays walks from the first date up to, but not including, the second one, and counts the working days: that is a half-open range, the same rule as in most loops. The year 2030 has 261 weekdays and 3 holidays on weekdays, so 258 working days. (Walking day by day is fast enough for a few years; for a very long range count whole weeks first: each week has 5 working days.)

ISO weeks#

The ISO week starts on Monday, and week 1 is the week with the first Thursday of the year. So 1 January can belong to the last week of the year before (1 January 2027 is in week 53 of 2026) and the last days of December can belong to week 1 of the next year (30 December 2030 is in week 1 of 2031). That is why the function gives the week and the year of the week: ISOWeek.GetWeekOfYear and ISOWeek.GetYear. A report by week uses the pair, not the calendar year.

Quarters and month ends#

The quarter of a month is floor((month - 1) / 3) + 1. DateTime.DaysInMonth(year, month) knows the leap years (February 2032 has 29 days), so the last day of a month is the date with that day number; no program should have a table of month lengths.

An age#

The age is the difference of the years, minus one if the birthday of this year has not come. Someone born on 9 March 2000 is 30 on 9 March 2030, and someone born the next day is still 29. Subtracting the days and dividing by 365 is wrong: the leap days add up.

What you learned#

  • the weekend and the holidays as data in one place,
  • adding and counting working days,
  • the ISO week and its year,
  • quarters, month lengths and ages.
